# Intelligence Is the Discovery of Compressors

**Intelligence is the discovery of compressors whose inductive bias matches the structure of
the data they meet.**

Not every compression is an act of intelligence, but every compressor was found by one, and
the discriminator is the distinction between the act and the artifact. Inventing the Fourier
transform, the Huffman code or least squares was the act; running the resulting algorithm
over a buffer replays a search that was completed once and has been amortized across every
invocation since. Between the two poles sits the case that recurs daily, judging which known
compressor fits the data at hand, which is discovery at reduced amplitude and intelligent in
the same sense, in smaller measure. Intelligence in compression is a gradient of
amortization, not a binary.

Bias and variance name the two cheap failures on either side of the useful band:
memorization of specific examples without generative structure, and over-smoothing that
loses the structure. What lies between is named by the word both are built from, fitting,
and it is an operation rather than a location.

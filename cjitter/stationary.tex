\begin{abstract}
A layout tool places the boxes of a diagram by minimising a weighted sum of aesthetic
criteria, and learning the weights from drawings people made has been proposed since
1994. Fitting the weights to the coordinates, as inverse optimisation does, assumes each
drawing is a local minimum of some weighting. We test that one criterion at a time: move
each box a fixed distance in sixteen directions, the rest fixed, and call the box held if
no move lowers the criterion; $q$ is the fraction of boxes held. A converged layout must
read near 1 or the test lacks power for that criterion, and a random layout says what
held costs by chance.

On 853 working diagrams of 15 to 40 boxes from three communities, laid out in their
editors by the people who made them (hand layouts below), overlap holds every box of the
median drawing at value zero. Uniform edge length and stress hold 0.00 of the
median drawing there, in 136 laboratory drawings and in 1,072 figures from this field's
proceedings, while a stress minimiser's own layouts are held at 1.00, and the hand
layouts' stress is five to twelve times the minimiser's; a weight of 0.001 on either term
beside crossings and overlap holds under 0.015 of a corpus's boxes, so no weighting of
the base terms with positive weight on a distance term holds a hand layout.
Offered the base energy, inverse optimisation returns all weight on overlap or crossings,
the terms every layout satisfies. What the coordinates do hold is alignment into rows and
columns: 58, 36 and 81 per cent of the median drawing's boxes lie in an alignment of three
or more, none of the stress minimiser's, and a pairwise alignment term holds, at sixteen
directions, 0.52, 0.21 and 0.85 of them against 0.06 to 0.07 by chance and 0.06 to 0.09 after snapping a random
layout to the same grid, the count tracking the share of boxes that share an exact
coordinate with another, 0.54, 0.17 and 0.87. Neither base energy, Davidson and Harel's
sum or stress, prices it. Whether the person aligned the boxes or the editor's guide did, the
coordinates cannot say, and for fitting it does not matter. Offer alignment among the
terms before any fit, and judge a fitted energy by the fraction of a held-out drawing's
boxes it holds under the terms no layout satisfies exactly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A layout program places the boxes of a diagram by minimising an \emph{energy}, a weighted
sum of quantities the field has agreed are bad \cite{tdb88, dh96, purchase02, kobourov},
and since 1994 the proposal has been to learn the weights from layouts people made or
preferred \cite{masui94, spoenemann14, klammler19, flud22, zhang26}. Learning from
preferences needs only that an accepted layout rank well under the learned energy;
fitting to the coordinates, which is what inverse optimisation does \cite{ahuja01,
keshavarz11, chan19}, what O'Donovan, Agarwala and Hertzmann did for single-page designs
\cite{odonovan14}, and what Flud \cite{flud22} rests on when it continues a descent from
crowd layouts, needs the coordinates themselves to sit at a local minimum. In graph
drawing that property has not been tested, 4,890 figures were just collected from this
field's proceedings into one corpus \cite{gdcoll25}, and this paper is the check to run
before the first fit: move each box of a drawing a small distance in sixteen directions,
call it \emph{held} if no move lowers a criterion, and report the fraction of boxes held,
$q$, per criterion, against a converged layout (which must read near 1) and a random one
(chance). Table~\ref{tab:profile} is the result: no distance term holds a hand-placed
box, no reweighting repairs it, and what the coordinates hold is an alignment into rows
and columns that the base energies omit and that the coordinates cannot attribute to the
hand rather than the editor's guide. Section~\ref{sec:test} is the test and what it
rests on, Section~\ref{sec:corpora} the corpora, Section~\ref{sec:results} the
measurements, Section~\ref{sec:alternatives} what else could produce them,
Section~\ref{sec:related} what was known and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} what follows
for a tool that learns its weights; the sensitivities are in a supplement and every
table's script in the repository.

\section{The test}\label{sec:test}

\input{figures/test}

\paragraph{The energy.} A layout of a graph with $N$ nodes is a point
$x \in \mathbb{R}^{2N}$, two coordinates per node, the boxes' sizes held fixed. An
aesthetic energy is
\begin{equation}
E_w(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k\, T_k(x), \qquad w_k \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where each term $T_k$ is a quantity to be made small. Multicriteria layout is the choice
of the $T_k$ and the $w_k$ \cite{dh96, purchase02}.

\paragraph{Held.} A point $x$ is a local minimum of $E$ if $E(y) \ge E(x)$ for every $y$
within some distance of $x$. Two of the base terms are not differentiable, crossings
being a count and overlap having corners where boxes touch, so the test uses no gradient.
Fix a radius $d$ and a set $V$ of sixteen equally spaced unit directions. For node $i$
and direction $v$ let $x + d\,v^{(i)}$ be the layout with node $i$ moved by $d$ along $v$
and every other node fixed (Figure~\ref{fig:test}). Node $i$ is \emph{held} at radius $d$
if
\begin{equation}
E(x + d\,v^{(i)}) \ge E(x) \quad \text{for every } v \in V,
\label{eq:held}
\end{equation}
and the estimand is
\begin{equation}
q_d(x) = \frac{1}{N}\,\#\{\,i : \text{node } i \text{ is held at radius } d\,\}.
\label{eq:q}
\end{equation}
$q_d = 1$ says the layout is a local minimum of $E$ over single-node moves of length $d$;
for smooth $E$ and small $d$ it says $\nabla E(x) = 0$ to within the resolution of $V$. A
person in a diagram editor moves one box at a time, so single-node moves are the editor's
own move set, and equation~\eqref{eq:held} compares two energies, so it is defined for
every term. Evaluated per term it separates the criteria a layout satisfies from those it
does not. The primary radius is $d = 0.02$ of the drawing width; a move that lowers the energy by less than
$10^{-12}$ of its value is a tie. The test is deterministic: no seed, no budget. The
tables report the median of $q$ over diagrams; the fit and the weight sweep report the
fraction of all boxes in a corpus held, which is lower wherever diagrams pile up at 1.

\paragraph{What $q$ counts.} A held fraction means different things for terms of
different shape. For a smooth term near a minimum with gradient $g$ and curvature
$\lambda$ along it, a node is held when $|g| < d\lambda/2$: $q$ reads how near the minimum
is. For a count (crossings) or a step function of position (gridiness, below), a node is
held wherever no move of length $d$ changes the term: $q$ reads how much of the layout the
term has stopped acting on, so the term's value is reported beside it. For a term with a
corner at exact coincidence (the alignment term A1 below), a node is held when it sits
within $0.19\,d$ of sharing a coordinate with another node
(Section~\ref{sec:alternatives}) and is free to slide along that row or column: $q$
counts boxes pinned in one coordinate, at a resolution the direction set fixes. Beside
$q$ the results report the median best decrease a single move finds, as a fraction of
the term's value, and the share of diagrams in which any box has an improving move.

\paragraph{The inverse problem.} Given layouts $x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(G)}$ that people
accepted, inverse optimisation asks for the weights $w$ under which they are nearest to
stationary \cite{keshavarz11, chan19}. With the directional differences
$D_{iv} T_k = T_k(x + d\,v^{(i)}) - T_k(x)$ in hand, node $i$ is held under $w$ exactly
when $\sum_k w_k D_{iv} T_k \ge 0$ for all $v$, a set of linear inequalities in $w$. The
residual is the smallest total violation over $w$ on the simplex, the normalisation that
keeps the trivial vertex in the feasible set, a linear program, and it is zero only if
some weighting makes the layout a local minimum. Two properties of that
program decide what a fit can return. A term with $D_{iv} T_k < 0$ for some $v$ at almost
every node can take positive weight only where another term rises in that direction;
under the base energy nothing else moves there, and the sweep of
Section~\ref{sec:results} measures how much weight such a term can carry. And a term
every layout satisfies exactly, its value zero so that $D_{iv} T_k \ge 0$ everywhere, is
a zero-residual vertex on its own: the program returns all weight on it and learns nothing. The second is
the degeneracy Ng and Russell \cite{ng00} state for inverse reinforcement learning, where a
zero reward makes every policy optimal; the repairs add a margin \cite{ratliff06} or take
demonstrations as only near-optimal \cite{ziebart08, keshavarz11, aswani18}. Under any
first-order fit neither rescues a term of the first kind, which carries no weight under
any margin; a contrastive fit, which reads a weight off the gap between a demonstration
and its alternatives, is a different estimator (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{The reference length.} The length and stress terms compare distances with a
reference $L$. Fixed at the layout's median edge length, $L$ is not the scale at which
the term is least, so at a true minimum every node still has a scaling direction that
lowers the term: \texttt{neato}'s converged stress layouts read $q = 0.07$ to $0.12$ under
their own criterion. $L$ is therefore the value at which the term is least for the layout
as loaded, $\sum \ell^2 / \sum \ell$ over edges for length and the same over
$\|x_i - x_j\| / \delta_{ij}$ for stress, fixed before any move; the converged layouts are
then held at $1.00$. This is the scale-normalised stress of Smelser, Miller and Kobourov
\cite{smelser24} and of Ahmed et al.\ \cite{ahmed24}, and fitting $L$ is their repair for
the pathology they report. The median and $1/\sqrt{N}$ are sensitivities
(Supplement~S3).

\paragraph{The controls.} A test that can only succeed is not a test. The
\emph{converged reference}: $q_d$ on a layout a descent has converged on must be near
one, or the test lacks power for that energy. \texttt{neato}'s layouts are the reference
for stress, and the end point of an uncapped descent from each hand layout is the
reference for every summed energy (median $q$ 1.00 in every corpus,
Supplement~S4). The \emph{tool controls}: the same graphs with the same box
sizes and edge directions laid out by \texttt{neato} (stress, \texttt{-Gstart=1}, boxes
left where they overlap), \texttt{prism} (\texttt{neato} followed by its overlap removal),
\texttt{dot} (layered) and ELK layered 0.12.0 \cite{schulze14}, the engine the BPMN
editors run; a check in the binary confirms each control is the same graphs. The
\emph{null}: a uniform random layout of the same graph in the same bounding box, and for
the alignment term a random layout snapped to the same grid as the drawing.

$L$ fitted to the layout under test:

The base energy is $T_C + T_O + T_L$ at weights $(1,1,1)$, three terms of the family that
runs from Davidson and Harel's simulated annealing \cite{dh96} to the gradient-descent
layouts of Ahmed et al.\ \cite{ahmed20}; stress \cite{kamada89, gansner05}, which force-directed tools and
\texttt{neato} minimise, is the second base energy. $T_R$, $T_A$ and $T_N$ are the
candidates for what the base energies miss. A1 has a corner at exact alignment; a smooth
Gaussian form, A3 with width 0.02, is the sensitivity. The reading direction $u$ is the
axis direction under which $T_F$ is least for the layout as loaded, fixed before any move,
the same rule as for $L$.

\paragraph{Routes.} An edge is the polyline the file stores, attachment points and
interior waypoints, or the chord between the centres where the file stores nothing.
Crossings, orthogonality and node--edge separation are evaluated on the route, length and
stress on the centres; when a node moves its attachment points move with it and the
waypoints stay. A waypoint is stored on 0.29, 0.07 and 0.33 of the edges by corpus, and
only the BPMN routes are orthogonal (0.88 axis-aligned at every segment, against 0.01 in
the biological formats). The chords are the sensitivity, and no verdict moves under them.

\paragraph{Fitting.} Weights on the simplex, the linear program above over every node and
direction of a corpus (about 110,000 inequalities, HiGHS), the residual its value per
node. Because the residual depends on the terms' scales and $q$ does not, $q$ under the
fitted weights is reported beside it, on the fitting diagrams and held out under
$5 \times 2$ cross-validation (five seeded halvings, each half fitted and scored on the
other). The sweep, $q$ as the weight on one term grows from 0 with the rest on crossings
and overlap, is the test of whether any positive weight on that term can be carried.

\paragraph{Statistics.} The unit of inference is the diagram. Every median carries a 95\%
bootstrap interval over diagrams (1,000 resamples); an interval of [0.00, 0.00] or [1.00,
1.00] is a pileup at the boundary, and the share of diagrams at exactly that value is
given where it matters. Hand against tool on one criterion is a Hodges--Lehmann shift
with its distribution-free interval (Supplement~S5); equivalence claims are read off the
sweep, never off a failure to reject. The tests are exploratory: the plan's hypotheses
were fixed after a pilot (Supplement~S8). The test and the terms are C in the repository; the
descents run through \texttt{cjitter}, a library of seeded searches with uniform sampling
at the same budget as its built-in control.

The first three are working documents: Reactome diagrams are laid out by curators,

sizes with it. Of the BPMN archive's 29,810 files, 21,784 parse as models with ten or more nodes, and

coordinates and by structure finds three pairs in the biological corpora and none in
BPMN, and removing them moves no median; the 305 WikiPathways pathways carry 153 first
authors, and the alignment median's interval clustered by author, [0.48, 0.55], is within 0.01
of the interval over diagrams (\texttt{results/dedup.py}, \texttt{results/drawers.py}).

The two further collections are drawings, not working diagrams. HOLA's formative
corpus is 17 participants each laying out the same 8 abstract graphs in the study's own
orthogonal editor, 136 drawings of 4 to 13 nodes, scraped from the study's published SVGs and redistributed
as research data without an explicit licence;

the test radius is a smaller fraction of a box than in the band (its distance-term zeros
stand at radii 0.01 and 0.05 too). The GD Collection is
4,890 figures harvested from 27 editions of this field's proceedings, 1,072 of them with
a largest component in the band; who or what made each figure is not recorded, and many
are the output of the algorithms the papers present. Its authors' audit reproduces 69\%
of figures exactly, edge directions and box sizes are absent, so only the terms needing
neither are run on it.

coordinates, 17\% are integers in WikiPathways, 67\% in Reactome and 63\% in BPMN; the
fraction of boxes sharing an exact $x$ with another box is 0.37, 0.06 and 0.41, an exact

WikiPathways exact alignment is twice as common as integer coordinates, so it is not a
rounding grid's act. PathVisio and the BPMN editors draw a snap-to-object guide on every
drag; Reactome's curation tool does not. Section~\ref{sec:alternatives} takes this up.

\section{What the coordinates hold}\label{sec:results}

\input{figures/tools}

Figure~\ref{fig:tools} shows one graph under the hand and three tools. The tables are
from one deterministic pass over the corpora (\texttt{results/run\_all.sh}; commit, versions, seeds and corpus hashes in
\texttt{results/manifest.txt}); the BPMN sample runs by the same scripts with

\paragraph{Each criterion alone.} Table~\ref{tab:profile}; \texttt{prism}, the A3 kernel
and the summed energies are in Supplement~S1.



Read down a hand column. Overlap holds every box of the median diagram at value zero, as
it does in every tool layout that removes overlaps; in 5 to 18 per cent of hand diagrams
some box has an overlap-lowering move, the overlap being positive but too small to print.
Length and stress hold no box of the median hand diagram, and 0.85 to 0.92 of diagrams
sit at exactly 0.00; \texttt{neato}'s stress layouts are held at 1.00 under the same
test, so the zero is the layout's and not the test's. The random layouts read 0.00 too,
as do \texttt{dot} and ELK, which are layered and price no distance: at this radius no
layout is stationary under a distance term except a minimiser's converged output, and
the hand layouts stand with the layered engines. Alignment is where the hand layout is held and \texttt{neato}'s is not. The gridiness value puts 58, 36 and 81 per cent of hand-placed boxes in an
alignment of three or more, none of \texttt{neato}'s, and 88, 82 and 76 per cent of
\texttt{dot}'s, since a layer is a row; the gridiness $q$ on \texttt{neato}'s layouts is
the step's flatness, so alignment is compared on the value, with the A1 hold beside it as
the count a fit needs. The A1 hold tracks the exact-tie fractions of
Section~\ref{sec:corpora} to within a few points, as a corner term should, and it is an
upper bound that falls with the direction count, to 0.39, 0.07 and 0.82 at sixty-four
against a chance line of 0.03, 0.03 and 0.00 (Supplement~S2); the gridiness value does
not move with it. Alignment does
not hold the most boxes: node--edge separation holds 0.88 to 1.00 and crossings and
overlap hold every box. What separates alignment from those three is that the base
energies price them and do not price it.

\section{What else could produce this}\label{sec:alternatives}

\input{figures/controls}

\paragraph{The editor snapped to a grid,} so alignment is the tool's. Excluded. The
detected pitch is one unit in Reactome and BPMN and none in WikiPathways, where exact
alignment is twice as common as integer coordinates. A layout snapped to the same grid,
with the same boxes and the same bounding box and no author, holds 0.06 to 0.09 over
three seeds in every corpus (Figure~\ref{fig:controls}); snapped to the plausible working
pitch of five units, 0.076 (first 300). The grid is available to the random layout and does not
produce the effect. Displacing every hand-placed box by up to half a pitch leaves the
hold within 0.02 of where it was, and that test cannot fail, for a reason in the
instrument: a box offset by $\epsilon$ from its nearest agreement is released only by a
move whose component toward the agreement, $a$, is less than $2\epsilon$, since the move
lands at $|\epsilon - a|$; the smallest such component among sixteen directions is
$0.38\,d$, so release begins at $\epsilon > 0.19\,d$, which is 3 to 5 units against a half
pitch of 0.5. Displacing past that takes the hand layouts to the snapped random level
(0.06 at a half-width of 120 units; Supplement~S7).

\paragraph{The editor snapped to the neighbours,} so alignment is the guide's. Not
separable on these corpora, and conceded: a grid control cannot see snap-to-object
guides, and the hold orders as the editors' guides of Section~\ref{sec:corpora} do,
highest where guides meet every drag and lowest where they are absent. Two pieces of
evidence say no single editor's aid produces it: HOLA's drawings come from a study tool
and hold 0.50, and WikiPathways is flat at 0.47 to 0.53 across four generations of
pathway numbers, so no gradient follows PathVisio's releases (\texttt{results/era.md}). Separating hand from guide needs a guide-free editor or an
interaction log, and these corpora carry neither. Marriott et al.\ \cite{marriott12}
bound the other side: in a recall task node alignment was the one feature participants
did not preserve, so the alignment these coordinates hold is a property of how the
layouts were made, not evidence that anyone perceived it. Every claim here is about the
coordinates as data for a fit, and for a fit the attribution changes nothing: the
coordinates hold a term the energy lacks either way.

\paragraph{The lanes hold the boxes.} 82\% of BPMN boxes sit in a lane or pool, and a
horizontal lane constrains $y$. Of the held boxes, 28\% are held by a column, which no
horizontal lane imposes, 14\% by a row shared across lanes, and 58\% by a row within one
lane, and those lanes are a median 6.3 box heights tall, so the row inside them was
chosen (\texttt{results/lanes.py}).

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Aesthetic criteria and their weighting go back to Tamassia, Di Battista and Batini
\cite{tdb88} and were made an explicit energy by Davidson and Harel \cite{dh96}. Purchase
\cite{purchase02} gave the criteria their metrics, among them node orthogonality, the fit
of node positions to an imaginary grid; Bennett et al.'s survey \cite{bennett07} lists it
and uniform edge length side by side, each with a Gestalt rationale and, as of 2007,
neither evaluated. The test here is such an evaluation, at the coordinates, and it
separates the two.

Alignment as a human criterion is known since 2012 in graph drawing \cite{purchase12}
and since 1985 in drawing editors: Pavlidis and Van Wyk's beautifier inferred alignment constraints from the
near-alignments of a rough drawing \cite{pvw85}, Igarashi et al.\ made the inference
interactive \cite{igarashi97}, and the guides of Section~\ref{sec:alternatives} are their
descendants. Energies and constraint systems that price it exist: HOLA's gridiness step
\cite{hola16}, O'Donovan's alignment terms \cite{odonovan14}, and stress under alignment
constraints \cite{ipsepcola06, dunnart08, setcola18}. The new part is the count of how
many hand-placed boxes it holds beside the counts for the criteria the base energies
weight, and the step under the distance terms. Van Wageningen, Mchedlidze and Telea
\cite{wageningen25} show drawings with identical metric values that differ in quality, so
a fitted energy with no residual would still not be the energy people use; the claims
here stay on the negative side. The inverse problem and its degeneracy are
Section~\ref{sec:test}'s, and Marriott et al.'s test of feature preservation against
random redrawings \cite{marriott12} is the nearest precedent in layout to the nulls used
here.

\section{What follows}\label{sec:discussion}

Coordinates a person accepted are not minima of the energy in use. The failure is
specific: overlap is met exactly, uniform edge length and stress are nowhere near met and
carry no weight from 0.001 up, and what holds the boxes instead is an alignment into rows and columns that neither base
energy prices. The charge is bounded twice: \texttt{dot} and ELK hold alignment at 0.42
to 1.00 without pricing it, since layering aligns as a side effect, so the missing term
convicts the energies and not the layered engines; and the energies that do price
alignment, HOLA's, O'Donovan's and the constraint systems, are not the ones a coordinate
fit to a graph-drawing corpus would be offered.

\paragraph{For a tool that learns its weights.} Offer alignment among the terms or the
fit returns nothing the drawing did. That is an addition, not a subtraction: edge length
and stress keep their weight, since a term inert at human coordinates is not thereby
unwanted, and the preference evidence runs the other way \cite{mooney25, dwyer09}. People
prefer the layouts a stress minimiser makes, do not draw that way, and do not notice the
alignment they do draw \cite{marriott12}; no study yet takes production and perception
from one corpus. An energy fitted to coordinates a snap guide produced learns the guide:
an editor that already snaps gains a term it enforces on every drag, and a layout engine
without a guide gains what the guide gives. The number to report for a fitted energy is
the fraction of a held-out drawing's boxes it holds under the terms no layout satisfies
exactly, $q_{0.02}$ at sixteen directions with the fitted $L$, with the decrease and
active columns beside it, since a bare held fraction is bought by weighting a term
everyone satisfies: the degenerate fit reads 0.99. The gain is small and measured: replacing each box in turn against the frozen rest,
alignment does not move the median distance to the drawing's position and raises the
share of boxes landing on the drawing's row or column by a fifth to a half
(\texttt{results/reinsert.md}).

\paragraph{Where the test applies.} Before any decomposable objective is fitted to
artefacts taken as optimal, wherever the analyst can move one variable at a time, the
per-term held fraction with a converged reference and a random null sorts the terms into
satisfied (held at value zero, the source of the trivial solution), inert (flat at the
demonstration radius) and violated (an improving move at almost every demonstration, so
no first-order fit can weight it), and a candidate term that holds the demonstrations
while the fitted energy holds none is a missing feature found without fitting. It does
not transfer to sequential decisions, whose condition is optimality along a trajectory.
A contrastive fit reads a different quantity: hand layouts carry half the stress of
random layouts of the same graphs (0.20 to 0.22 against 0.44 to 0.51) and more length
non-uniformity (0.22 to 0.28 against 0.19 to 0.22, \texttt{results/nulls.md}), so a
preference or likelihood fit would weight stress and not length uniformity, where the
coordinate fit weights neither.

\paragraph{Limits.} The alignment count is specific to box-and-arrow diagrams of 4 to 100
boxes drawn in editors with guides, it falls with size, and its level is stated at
sixteen directions, where the corner's resolution is $0.19\,d$. The hand is not separated
from the guide. The GD Collection is measured on chord edges with no box sizes and does
not record who made its figures. The distance terms read 0.00 on every layout that is
not a minimiser's output, so the negative result separates hand layouts from converged
ones and from nothing else. The BPMN corpus is a sample of 300 from 6,723. The
first-order residual is a scale-dependent number and is reported only beside $q$.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

Parsers, the energy, the directional test, the descent, the fitting scripts, the parsed
corpora and every table's script are in the repository under \texttt{example/diagrams},
each analysis beside its committed output, and \texttt{results/manifest.txt} records the
commit, the tool versions, the seeds and the SHA-256 of the raw archives and the corpus
files. The directional test is deterministic and the descents are deterministic from
their seed. The plan the study was run to is beside the paper with its outcomes in
Supplement~S8, and the supplement is \texttt{stationary\_supplement.pdf} beside this file.

\begin{abstract}
Fitting the weights of a layout energy to the coordinates of layouts people made assumes
each layout is a local minimum of some weighting of the criteria. Per criterion, move
each box 0.02 of the drawing width in sixteen directions and count the boxes no move
improves, against a converged layout and a random one. On 853 working diagrams of 15 to 40
boxes from three editors, overlap holds every box. Uniform edge length and stress hold
none, there and in 136 laboratory drawings and 1,072 figures from the Graph Drawing
proceedings. A stress minimiser's own layouts are held at 1.00, and the hand layouts carry
five to twelve times the minimiser's stress. A weight of 0.001 on either term beside
crossings and overlap holds under 0.015 of the boxes. Two criteria the base energies omit
do hold the working diagrams. Alignment: 58, 36 and 81 per cent of boxes sit in a row or
column of three or more, against 4 to 7 by chance. Flow: 0.59 to 0.86 of boxes are held,
against 0.28 to 0.41. Person or editor, the coordinates cannot say which aligned them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A layout program places the boxes of a diagram by minimising an \emph{energy}, a weighted
sum of quantities the field has agreed are bad~\cite{tdb88, dh96, purchase02, kobourov};
a term that carries a criterion in the energy is said below to \emph{price} it. Where an
objective is fitted to what people did, the standard estimator takes the demonstration as
a stationary point of the unknown objective~\cite{ahuja01}: inverse optimisation
minimises the residual of the optimality conditions at the observed decision
\cite{keshavarz11, chan19},
inverse optimal control of manipulation~\cite{englert17} and of arm movement
\cite{berret11} assumes the demonstrations satisfy those conditions, and inverse
reinforcement learning assumes the demonstrated policy optimal~\cite{ng00} or locally so
\cite{levine12}. In layout the published fit of this kind is O'Donovan, Agarwala and
Hertzmann's energy for single-page designs, each example taken as optimal
\cite{odonovan14}, and a reward fitted to expert chip placements rests on the same premise
\cite{chen26}. Graph drawing has learned weights from preferences, selections and labels
since 1994~\cite{masui94, spoenemann14, klammler19, zhang26}, which needs only that an
accepted layout rank well, and has imitated the coordinates of algorithm output
\cite{smartgd24, deepdrawing19}; no published graph-drawing method fits an energy to
human coordinates. The corpus to do it on now exists, 4,890 figures from this field's
proceedings~\cite{gdcoll25}, and Flud~\cite{flud22} already continues a descent from crowd
layouts of pathway diagrams. This paper is the check to run before that fit.

Move each box of a drawing a small distance in sixteen directions with the rest fixed;
call the box \emph{held} if no move lowers a criterion; report the fraction of boxes held,
$q$, per criterion. Two controls: a converged layout, which checks the test's power, and a
random layout, which gives the chance line. Run before any weight is fitted, the count
sorts the terms into satisfied, inert and violated at the demonstrations, and a term that
holds the demonstrations while the fitted energy holds none is a missing feature found
without fitting. The fitting literature, when it measures the demonstrations' distance
from optimality, does so inside the fit and in aggregate~\cite{chan19}.
Table~\ref{tab:profile} is the result on three working corpora, the layouts people made in
their editors (hand layouts below), against three tool controls and a random null. The new
part is the per-term held count at human coordinates with those controls, the drop to
under 0.015 held at any weight on a distance term, and the profile, the vector of $q$ per
term, shared by hand layouts and layered engines.

\section{The test}\label{sec:test}

\input{figures/test}

\paragraph{The energy.} A layout of a graph with $N$ nodes is a point
$x \in \mathbb{R}^{2N}$, two coordinates per node, the boxes' sizes kept fixed. An
aesthetic energy is
\begin{equation}
E_w(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k\, T_k(x), \qquad w_k \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where each term $T_k$ is a quantity to be made small. Multicriteria layout is the choice
of the $T_k$ and the $w_k$ \cite{dh96, purchase02}.

\paragraph{Held.} A point $x$ is a local minimum of $E$ if $E(y) \ge E(x)$ for every $y$
within some distance of $x$. Two of the base terms are not differentiable, crossings
being a count and overlap having corners where boxes touch, so the test uses no gradient.
Fix a radius $d$ and a set $V$ of sixteen equally spaced unit directions. For node $i$
and direction $v$ let $x + d\,v^{(i)}$ be the layout with node $i$ moved by $d$ along $v$
and every other node fixed (Figure~\ref{fig:test}). Node $i$ is \emph{held} at radius $d$
if
\begin{equation}
E(x + d\,v^{(i)}) \ge E(x) \quad \text{for every } v \in V,
\label{eq:held}
\end{equation}
and the estimand is
\begin{equation}
q_d(x) = \frac{1}{N}\,\#\{\,i : \text{node } i \text{ is held at radius } d\,\}.
\label{eq:q}
\end{equation}
$q_d = 1$ says the layout is a local minimum of $E$ over single-node moves of length $d$,
a weaker property than a minimum over joint moves at every small radius, and the one
meant wherever this paper says minimum; for smooth $E$ and small $d$ it says
$\nabla E(x) = 0$ to within the resolution of $V$. A person in a diagram editor moves one
box at a time, so single-node moves are the editor's own move set, and
equation~\eqref{eq:held} compares two energies, so it is defined for every term. The
primary radius is $d = 0.02$ of the drawing width, the larger extent of its bounding box
(width below); a move that lowers the energy by less than $10^{-12}$ of its value counts
as no change. The test is deterministic: no seed, no budget.

\paragraph{Three quantities.} The tables report the \emph{median $q$} over diagrams,
called the \emph{hold} of a term below. The fit and the weight sweep report the
\emph{pooled $q$}, the fraction of all boxes in a corpus held. Pooled $q$ is below the
median wherever diagrams pile up at 1: in the sweep's alignment row WikiPathways reads
0.52 by the median and 0.506 pooled. The third quantity is the \emph{value} of a term at
the layout, 0 when satisfied. A held fraction means different
things for terms of different shape. For a smooth term near a minimum with gradient $g$
and curvature $\lambda$ along it, a node is held when $|g| < d\lambda/2$: $q$ reads how
near the minimum is. For a count (crossings) or a step function of position (gridiness),
a node is held wherever no move of length $d$ changes the term: $q$ reads how much of the
layout the term has stopped acting on, so the value is compared instead. For a term with
a corner at exact coincidence (the alignment term A1), a node is held when it sits within
$0.19\,d$ of sharing a coordinate with another node (Supplement~S2) and is free to slide
along that row or column: $q$ counts boxes pinned in one coordinate, at a resolution the
direction set fixes.

\paragraph{The inverse problem.} Given layouts $x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(G)}$ that people
accepted, the first-order fit asks for the weights $w$ under which they are nearest to
stationary, minimising the residual of the optimality conditions at the demonstrations
\cite{keshavarz11, englert17}. With the directional differences
$D_{iv} T_k = T_k(x + d\,v^{(i)}) - T_k(x)$ in hand, node $i$ is held under $w$ exactly
when $\sum_k w_k D_{iv} T_k \ge 0$ for all $v$, a set of linear inequalities in $w$. The
residual is the smallest total violation over $w$ on the simplex (the normalisation that
keeps the trivial vertex feasible). Finding it is a linear program, and it is zero only if
some weighting holds every box. Two properties of that program decide what the fit can
return. A term with $D_{iv} T_k < 0$ for some $v$ at almost every node can take positive
weight only where another term rises in that direction; under the base energy nothing
else rises there, and the sweep of Section~\ref{sec:results} measures how much weight
such a term can carry. And a term every layout satisfies exactly, its value zero so that
$D_{iv} T_k \ge 0$ everywhere, is a zero-residual vertex on its own: the program returns
all weight on it and learns nothing, the degeneracy Ng and Russell~\cite{ng00} state for
inverse reinforcement learning, where a zero reward makes every policy optimal. Aswani,
Shen and Siddiq~\cite{aswani18} prove the residual estimators inconsistent under noisy
demonstrations; the margin~\cite{ratliff06} and maximum-entropy~\cite{ziebart08} repairs
read a gap between the demonstration and its alternatives, a different quantity, and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} says what they would return here.

\paragraph{The reference length.} The length and stress terms compare distances with a
reference $L$. Fixed at the layout's median edge length, $L$ is not the scale at which
the term is least, so a true minimum still has a scaling move that lowers it:
\texttt{neato}'s converged stress layouts read $q = 0.07$ to $0.12$ under their own
criterion, and that observation fixed the rule. $L$ is fixed before any move at the length
that makes the term least for the layout as loaded: $\sum \ell^2 / \sum \ell$ over edges
for length, and the same over $\|x_i - x_j\| / \delta_{ij}$ for stress. This is the
scale-normalised stress of Smelser, Miller and Kobourov~\cite{smelser24} and of Ahmed et
al.~\cite{ahmed24}. The converged layouts are then held at 1.00; the hand verdicts are
0.00 to 0.05 under every rule tried (Supplement~S3).

\paragraph{The controls.} The \emph{converged reference}: $q_d$ on a layout a descent
has converged on must be near one, or the test lacks power for that energy.
\texttt{neato}, an independent minimiser of stress, is the power control: its layouts are
held at 1.00 under stress and the length term is of the same family. The end point of an
uncapped descent from each hand layout is the reference for every summed energy (median
$q$ 1.00 in every corpus, Supplement~S4), and since that climber proposes the test's own
single-box moves, this reference certifies the code and not the power. The \emph{tool controls}: the same graphs with the
same box sizes and edge directions laid out by \texttt{neato} (stress,
\texttt{-Gstart=1}, boxes left where they overlap), \texttt{prism} (\texttt{neato}
followed by its overlap removal), \texttt{dot} (layered) and ELK layered 0.12.0
\cite{schulze14}, run as \texttt{elkjs} at the library's defaults (node placement
Brandes--K\"opf with edge straightening, direction right, seed 1), not as any of the
editors that offer it configure it. Graphviz is the Ubuntu 24.04 package 2.42.2, which
reports itself as 2.43.0, built with GTS so that \texttt{-Goverlap=false} is
\texttt{prism}. The tools receive the graph without its containers and with undirected
edges in stored order; their routes are discarded, so crossings, orthogonality and
node--edge separation are read on chords in the tool columns and on the stored routes in
the hand column, and Supplement~S3 gives the hand column on chords. A check in the binary
confirms that each control lays out the same graphs; the command lines are
\texttt{data/make\_corpus.py} and \texttt{data/elk\_layout.py}. The \emph{null}: a uniform random layout of
the same graph in the same bounding box, whose $q$ is the chance line, and for the
alignment term a random layout snapped to the same grid as the drawing.

\paragraph{Terms.} Table~\ref{tab:terms}. The base energy is $T_C + T_O + T_L$ at weights
$(1,1,1)$, three terms of the family that runs from Davidson and Harel's simulated
annealing~\cite{dh96} to the gradient-descent layouts of Ahmed et al.\ \cite{ahmed20};
stress~\cite{kamada89, gansner05}, which force-directed tools and \texttt{neato}
minimise, is the second base energy. $T_R$, $T_A$ and $T_N$ are the candidates for what
the base energies miss. A1 has a corner at exact alignment and A3 has none; A1 is the primary
term, A3 the sensitivity check (the binary defaults to A3, so every run here passes
\texttt{--align a1}). The reading direction $u$ is the axis direction
under which $T_F$ is least for the layout as loaded, fixed before any move, the same rule
as for $L$.

\paragraph{Fitting.} The weights lie on the simplex; the linear program above runs over
every node and direction of a corpus (100,000 to 120,000 inequalities per corpus, solved
with HiGHS), and the residual is its optimum per node. Because the residual depends on the
terms' scales and $q$ does not, the pooled $q$ under the fitted weights is reported beside
it, on the fitting diagrams and on held-out diagrams under $5 \times 2$ cross-validation
(five seeded halvings, each half fitted and scored on the other). The sweep, pooled $q$ as the weight on one term grows from 0 with
the rest on crossings and overlap, is the test of whether any positive weight on that
term can be carried.

\paragraph{Statistics.} The unit of inference is the diagram. Every median carries a 95\%
bootstrap interval over diagrams (1,000 resamples); an interval of [0.00, 0.00] or [1.00,
1.00] is a pileup at the boundary, and the share of diagrams at exactly that $q$ is given
where it matters. Hand against tool on one criterion is a Hodges--Lehmann shift with its
large-sample interval, pairs that agree dropped (Supplement~S5);
equivalence claims are read off the sweep, never off a failure to reject. The tests are
exploratory: the plan's hypotheses were fixed after a pilot (Supplement~S8).

\section{What the coordinates hold}\label{sec:results}

\input{figures/tools}

\paragraph{Length and stress.} The median hand diagram holds no box under either term in
any corpus, and 0.85 to 0.92 of diagrams sit at exactly 0.00, the [0.00, 0.00] intervals
of Table~\ref{tab:profile} (\texttt{results/pileups.md}).
By itself the zero says little: a smooth term is stationary at almost no point a descent
did not choose, and at this radius every layout that is not a minimiser's converged
output reads 0.00, the random layouts, \texttt{dot} and ELK included. Three measurements
fix what the zero is worth. \texttt{neato}'s stress layouts are held at 1.00 under the
same test and stay held until every box has moved about the test radius: displaced by
0.005, 0.01, 0.02 and 0.05 of the width they read 0.94 to 0.96, 0.35 to 0.39, 0.05 to
0.06 and 0.00 (Supplement~S4), so a layout at 0.00 sits more than 0.02 of the width from a
minimum by the test. The hand layouts' stress value, 0.20 to 0.22, is five to
twelve times the converged value (0.016 to 0.042) and under half a random layout's (0.44
to 0.51). And every diagram has improving moves (Table~\ref{tab:dec}), the mean best one
worth 1.3 to 1.4 per cent of the length term and 0.5 to 0.7 per cent of stress, and none
was taken; one such move is below perception, the departure is cumulative, and a climber
capped at the test radius removes 0.28 to 0.31 of $C{+}O{+}L$ and 0.12 to 0.15 of stress
(Supplement~S4). The hand layouts stand with the layered engines, which price neither length uniformity
nor stress.

\paragraph{Overlap and crossings.} Overlap holds every box of the median diagram at value
zero, as it does in every tool layout that removes overlaps; in 5 to 18 per cent of hand
diagrams some box has an overlap-lowering move, the overlap positive and rounding to
0.000 of the box area. Crossings hold every box in every layout, and on BPMN only 3 per
cent of diagrams offer a crossing-reducing move at all, so ``crossings hold every box'' is
a statement about the radius and these near-trees ($m/n$ 1.04 to 1.11), not about the
author. Orthogonality reads the stored routes:
its 0.73 in BPMN is Signavio's router, which draws 0.87 of edges axis-aligned at every
segment, and a jitter of two units halves it (Supplement~S7).

\paragraph{Alignment.} Alignment is where the hand layout is held and \texttt{neato}'s
is not. The gridiness value puts 58, 36 and 81 per cent of hand-placed boxes in an
alignment of three or more, none of \texttt{neato}'s, 4 to 7 per cent of a random
layout's and 3 to 9 per cent of a grid-snapped random layout's, and 88, 82 and 76 per cent of
\texttt{dot}'s, since a layer is a row. That value is the alignment number of this paper:
it is kernel-free and does not move with the direction count. The A1 hold, 0.52, 0.21 and
0.85 against 0.06 to 0.07 by chance, is the count a first-order fit sees, and it is a
tie counter at the instrument's resolution: it tracks the exact-tie fractions of
Section~\ref{sec:corpora} to within 0.04, as a corner term should, and it is an upper
bound that falls with the direction count, to 0.39, 0.07 and 0.82 at sixty-four against
a chance line of 0.03, 0.03 and 0.00 (Supplement~S2). Alignment does not hold the most
boxes: node--edge separation holds 0.88 to 1.00 and crossings and overlap hold every
box. The base energies price those three and not alignment.

\paragraph{Flow.} Flow is the second criterion the base energies omit. It holds 0.59,
0.62 and 0.86 of hand-placed boxes against 0.28 to 0.41 by chance, and \texttt{dot} and
ELK hold it at 1.00 with the term at zero: the criterion the layered engines price
exactly, the person partly satisfies, tolerating a backward edge. In WikiPathways and
Reactome flow holds more boxes than A1 does.

\paragraph{Reweighting.} Table~\ref{tab:fit} is the linear program of
Section~\ref{sec:test} on four sets of terms, and Table~\ref{tab:sweep} the sweep. The
program is degenerate as that section says it must be: offered the base energy it puts
all weight on the term every layout satisfies and holds 0.99 of held-out boxes, and
offered all eight terms it does the same in the biological corpora, the hold on held-out
diagrams swinging across WikiPathways splits as zero-residual vertices tie differently; in
BPMN a vertex with 0.81 on alignment wins by the terms' scales alone and loses to
crossings when the crossings column of the differences is halved. Confined to length and
stress it holds 0.003 to 0.011 of the boxes. Confined to the four terms the base energies
do not price it is identified in every corpus, 0.88 to 0.95 on alignment. No energy
offered here makes the hand layouts minima: the best hold on held-out diagrams under
those four terms is 0.65 in BPMN, 0.25 in WikiPathways and 0.15 in Reactome. The fitted
four-term energy holds fewer WikiPathways boxes (0.248) than A1 alone beside crossings and
overlap (0.516): the residual depends on the terms' scales, and the 0.05 of weight the fit
spreads on the other three terms releases half the boxes the corner held. The
identification is the corner's, not the criterion's: under the smooth A3 kernel the same
four-term fit puts 0.57 to 0.58 on flow and 0.04 to 0.08 on alignment in WikiPathways and
BPMN, at a hold on held-out diagrams within 0.03 of the corner's, and all weight on
node--edge with an unstable hold in Reactome (Supplement~S1). A term satisfied exactly by
a fraction of the boxes, and possibly by the editor's aid, is a constraint the
environment imposes rather than a criterion the drawer trades off. The
learning-from-demonstration literature infers such constraints as their own object, the
cost weights given or recovered beside them~\cite{chou20, scobee20}; the tied boxes are
for A1 what every box is for overlap, a zero-residual set the program is drawn to.

\section{What else could produce this}\label{sec:alternatives}

\input{figures/controls}

\paragraph{The editor snapped to a grid,} so alignment is the tool's. Excluded. The
detected pitch is one unit in Reactome and BPMN and none in WikiPathways, where exact
alignment is twice as common as integer coordinates. A random layout snapped to the
drawing's unit grid (pitch one where none is detected), with the same boxes and the same
bounding box and no author, holds 0.06 to 0.09 over three seeds in every corpus
(Figure~\ref{fig:controls}); snapped to the plausible working pitch of five units, 0.076
(first-300 BPMN). The grid is available to the random layout and does not produce the
effect. Displacing every hand-placed box by up to half a pitch leaves the hold within
0.03 of where it was, and that test cannot fail: the corner releases a box only once its
offset from its nearest agreement exceeds $0.19\,d$ (Supplement~S2), 3 to 5 units
against a half pitch of 0.5. Displacing past that takes the hand layouts to the snapped
random level (0.06 at a half-width of 120 units; Supplement~S7).

\paragraph{The editor aligned the boxes,} by a snap guide or an align command. Not
separable on these corpora, and conceded: a grid control cannot see either. The hold is
highest, 0.85, in the one editor with a drag guide, Signavio. The other two editors draw
no guide, and there the exact ties came from the hand or from an align command applied to
a selection: WikiPathways holds 0.52 and Reactome 0.21 under align commands of the same
kind, so the aid does not fix the level. The hold appears under four editors that share
no code, HOLA's Orthowontist included at 0.50, and is flat across four generations of
WikiPathways pathway numbers (0.47 to 0.53, Supplement~S7), so it is not one editor's
artefact. Separating the hand from the align command needs an interaction log, and these
corpora carry none. Marriott et al.\ \cite{marriott12} bound the other side: in a
recall task node alignment was among the features participants did not preserve, so the
alignment these coordinates hold is a property of how the layouts were
made, not evidence that anyone perceived it. Every claim here is about the coordinates as data for
a fit, and for a fit the attribution changes nothing: the coordinates hold a term the
energy lacks either way.

\paragraph{The lanes place the boxes.} Of BPMN boxes, 82 per cent sit in a lane or pool,
and a horizontal lane constrains $y$. Of the held boxes, 28 per cent are held by a column,
which no horizontal lane imposes, 14 per cent by a row shared across lanes, and 58 per cent
by a row within one lane, and those lanes are a median 6.3 box heights tall, so the row
inside them was chosen.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Aesthetic criteria and their weighting go back to Tamassia, Di Battista and Batini
\cite{tdb88} and were made an explicit energy by Davidson and Harel~\cite{dh96}. Purchase
\cite{purchase02} gave the criteria their metrics, among them node orthogonality, the fit
of node positions to an imaginary grid; Bennett et al.'s survey~\cite{bennett07} lists it
and uniform edge length side by side, each with a Gestalt rationale and, as of 2007,
neither evaluated. The test here is such an evaluation, at the coordinates, and it
separates the two. Table~\ref{tab:related} is what was known, by how it was asked.

The learned quality scores~\cite{kwon18, haleem19, huang16} and Barbosa and Barreto's
genetic algorithm, which co-evolved a weight vector against a ranking users
supplied~\cite{barbosa01}, sit with Masui and Klammler: none tests whether an example is a minimum of the
learned score. Alignment as a human criterion has been known since 2012 in graph
drawing~\cite{purchase12} and since 1985 in drawing editors, where Pavlidis and Van Wyk's
beautifier inferred alignment constraints from the near-alignments of a rough
drawing~\cite{pvw85}, Igarashi et al.\ made the inference interactive~\cite{igarashi97},
and the guides and align commands of Section~\ref{sec:alternatives} are their
descendants. Energies and constraint systems that price it exist: HOLA's gridiness
step~\cite{hola16}, O'Donovan's alignment terms~\cite{odonovan14}, and stress under
alignment constraints~\cite{ipsepcola06, dunnart08, setcola18}. Van Wageningen,
Mchedlidze and Telea~\cite{wageningen25} show drawings with identical metric values that
differ in quality, so a fitted energy with no residual would still not be the energy
people use; the claims here stay on the negative side. Marriott et al.'s test of feature
preservation against random redrawings~\cite{marriott12} is the nearest precedent in
layout to the nulls used here.

\section{What follows}\label{sec:discussion}

The charge against the energies is bounded twice: \texttt{dot} and ELK hold alignment at
0.42 to 1.00 without a term for it, since a layer puts its nodes on one line and
Brandes--K\"opf straightens edges across layers, so the missing term convicts the
energies and not the layered engines; and the energies that do price
alignment, HOLA's, O'Donovan's and the constraint systems, are not the ones a coordinate
fit to a graph-drawing corpus would be offered.

\paragraph{For a tool that learns its weights.} Offer alignment among the terms, and flow
where the graph is directed, or the fit learns nothing of what the drawer did. That is an
addition: edge length and stress keep their weight, since a term inert at human
coordinates is not thereby unwanted, and the preference evidence runs the other way
\cite{mooney25, dwyer09}. People prefer the layouts a stress minimiser makes, do not draw
that way, and in recall do not preserve the alignment they do draw~\cite{marriott12}; no
study yet takes production and perception from one corpus. An energy fitted to
coordinates a snap guide or an align command produced learns the aid: an editor that
already snaps gains a term it enforces on every drag, and a layout engine without a guide
gains what the aid gives. The number to report for a fitted energy is the fraction of a held-out drawing's
boxes the energy holds under the terms whose median value at the demonstrations is above
zero: $q_{0.02}$ at sixteen directions with the fitted $L$, and the decrease and active
columns beside it. A bare held fraction is bought by weighting a term everyone satisfies,
as the degenerate fit of Section~\ref{sec:results} shows. For the corner term the
exact-tie share of Table~\ref{tab:ties} is the ceiling. Any tool that emits
\texttt{dot} with positions can report the number:
\texttt{data/dot2corpus.py drawing.gv my.txt}, then \texttt{station direct --corpus
my.txt --weights 0,0,0,0,0,1,0,0 --align a1}. The gain is small and measured: when each box is replaced in turn against the frozen
rest, alignment does not move the median distance to the drawing's position and raises
the share of boxes landing on the drawing's row or column by a fifth to a half
(\texttt{results/reinsert.md}). A preference or likelihood fit reads a different
quantity: hand layouts carry half the stress of random layouts of the same graphs and
more length non-uniformity (0.22 to 0.28 against 0.19 to 0.22), so such a fit would
weight stress and not length uniformity, where the coordinate fit weights neither. For a
layered engine, whose parameters are spacings and placement strategies rather than
weights, the result is a target level: on BPMN 81 per cent of hand-placed boxes sit in an
alignment of three or more and 72 per cent of ELK's, on Reactome 36 against 69, and the
levers are the node placement strategy and the straight-edge preference, none of which
targets an alignment fraction.

\paragraph{Limits.} The alignment count is specific to box-and-arrow diagrams of 4 to 100
boxes drawn in editors; it falls with size; its level is stated at sixteen directions.
Which unpriced criterion the four-term fit names depends on the kernel offered. The
negative result separates hand layouts from converged ones and from nothing else; its
content is the distance and the step. The term set is the force-directed family's.
Untested: a minimum node gap (which every layered engine prices, and which is live where
overlap is degenerate), total edge length, compactness, bends, symmetry and the edge
alignment of unequal boxes. Whether a layout started from an automatic one the person then
edited, the production case for a shipped editor, is not recorded; Reactome's Curator Tool
has a hierarchical layout command, so its diagrams may have, and HOLA's participants
started from a supplied layout. The tool controls are laid out without the containers the
person worked inside, and three of their eight terms are read on chords. The per-drawer spread of Sections~\ref{sec:corpora} and~\ref{sec:results} says a
population weight may be the wrong object. The BPMN authors' courses are not recorded, so
diagrams from one assignment may not be independent. The plan the study started from is
beside the paper, its outcomes in Supplement~S8.

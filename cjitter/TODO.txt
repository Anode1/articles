C. CLAIMS TO PROTECT
--------------------

The central claim is NOT:

"People prefer alignment."

The stronger and more defensible claim is:

"Human-accepted layouts are locally held by some conventional criteria
(overlap), are not locally held by the distance criteria that dominate
standard force-directed energies (length/stress), and show substantial
local stationarity under alignment criteria that those energies omit."

The paper should emphasize:

- per-node local stationarity;
- criterion-level decomposition;
- controls with the same graphs and box sizes;
- stress control demonstrating test power;
- alignment as the principal missing criterion;
- the distinction between satisfying a criterion and being held by a
  flat/step-function criterion;
- the negative nature of the inverse-optimisation conclusion.


D. FINAL CONCLUSION SHOULD NOT OVERCLAIM
----------------------------------------

Keep:

"take the weight off edge length, add alignment, and print the fraction
of boxes the energy holds when started from a layout a person accepted."

But qualify it if the final experiments show that flow, containment,
lanes, route geometry, or other criteria explain part of the result.

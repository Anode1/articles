## The abstention hypothesis

Classic blackboard systems worked because abstention was architectural: a
knowledge source fired only when its condition matched, so the board held
only justified claims, and a stuck contributor contributed silence. Human
blackboard collaborators behave the same way for reputation reasons: a
person will say "I cannot advance this" rather than post a guess. LLM
seats invert the default: they always produce something. Observed so far:
0 abstentions in 105 solo attempts (37 screen + 68 phase-1); all three
pilot-board seats posted confident diagnoses within 11 s, none hedged.

Predictions:

1. Phase-1 BOARD failures will show a confident wrong claim posted early
   and inherited by peers, rather than three seats stuck in silence.
2. A board brief adding an abstention norm ("a wrong claim costs more
   than silence; post what you verified, state what you do not know,
   stop; another seat continues") will outperform the standard brief at
   matched budget. One sentence is the intervention scale the retired
   hsearch project measured as dominant.
3. If (2) holds, the gain will exceed the HOMO-vs-SOLO gain, i.e. the
   norm matters more than the board.

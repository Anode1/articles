# Provenance stamp: Wikipedia publication of the binary "atree" format

**Recorded fact:** Vasili Gavrilov (the author) added the binary **atree** notation to English
Wikipedia as a genealogical numbering format, listing it among the existing genealogy formats, on
**8 November 2011**, one day after publishing its specification on LiveJournal (7 November 2011).

**What was added:** a new **"atree"** subsection under *Ascending numbering systems*, placing the
binary M/F (father/mother) ancestor-path notation alongside the existing genealogical numbering
systems (Ahnentafel / Sosa-Stradonitz, Henry, d'Aboville, …). The added text gives the M/F notation,
the subject-sex rule ("for a male root the first digit is always M, to avoid trimming zeros"), and a
comparison table mapping atree identifiers to Ahnentafel decimal and binary forms for female- and
male-root subjects, i.e. the author's atree specification verbatim.

**Context:** the article had **no edits in the preceding two months**, so 2011-11-08 is the section's
genuine first appearance, not a later addition (refuting any "added years later" claim).

## Why these anonymous IP edits are the author's

1. **Timing:** one day after the author's LiveJournal specification (2011-11-07, handle `siberean`).
2. **Place:** both IPs geolocate to **Ontario, Canada** (Toronto; Brampton on **Rogers**, the
   author's home ISP); the author was the only researcher working with the notation in Ontario.
3. **Content:** the added text is the author's atree spec verbatim, including the idiosyncratic
   male-root "first digit M" rule that matches his own converter logic.
4. **Logged-out editing:** the edits were made before the author registered a Wikipedia account,
   which is why they appear under IP addresses rather than a username (so a search by name finds
   nothing).
5. **Only other contributor elsewhere:** the one other early user of the bare notation,
   *MitchGlitch*, worked from **Quebec**, not Ontario.

# Conditional Probability in Diagnostic Testing

**One calculation in four notations, set out as an explicit isomorphism: the tree diagram of
decision analysis, Bayes' theorem, the 2x2 contingency table of epidemiology, and the
ARR/RRR pair of clinical risk reporting.**

Diagnostic results are systematically misread by patients and clinicians alike, though the
probability behind them is elementary. Gigerenzer and Hoffrage (1995) documented the
cognitive equivalence of natural-frequency representations such as the tree and the table.
That literature does not set the correspondence out as an isomorphism, nor extend it to the
ARR/RRR notation of clinical-trials reporting.

What the paper adds: a Diagnostic Interpretation Procedure, a short auditable algorithm
whose final step runs three sanity checks aimed at the documented failure modes (arithmetic
error, base-rate neglect, relative-versus-absolute confusion), with a flat spreadsheet
realization of the multi-test extension; and the argument that medical and epidemiology
training should teach the calculation through all four notations, not the contingency table
alone.
